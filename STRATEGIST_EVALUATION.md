# AI Marketing Strategist Evaluation & Benchmark Suite (STRATEGIST_EVALUATION.md)

## 1. Overview & Evaluation Philosophy

This evaluation suite defines the behavioral benchmark criteria used to audit, test, and validate the strategic rigor, economic discipline, positioning integrity, business model adaptation, and trade-off quality of the **Marketing Strategist & Growth Architect**.

The Strategist acts as the strategic engine that converts evidence into defensible choices. These 25 deterministic benchmark scenarios evaluate how the Strategist handles positioning trade-offs, business model economics, intentional strategic investments, brand vs demand balance, segmentation choices, channel selection, growth loops, and hypothesis design.

## 2. The 25 Strategic Benchmark Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Target-Everyone Strategy
- **INPUT**:
  User/CMO: *"Our new B2B project management software is useful for everyone from freelance designers to Fortune 500 enterprise teams. Create a universal marketing strategy that targets all of them simultaneously."*
- **EXPECTED STRATEGIST BEHAVIOR**:
  - Firmly rejects the "target-everyone" mandate as a strategic failure.
  - Explains the trade-off: A message that speaks to everyone resonates with no one; enterprise procurement needs directly conflict with solo freelancer self-serve onboarding.
  - Recommends selecting a single high-pain beachhead segment (e.g., 10–50 person creative agencies experiencing client review chaos) to achieve initial traction and positive unit economics.
  - Defines explicit non-pursuits (What We Will NOT Do: enterprise RFPs, solo student plans).
- **FAILURE CONDITIONS**:
  - Produces a generic strategy trying to address freelancers, SMBs, and enterprise teams in one campaign.
  - Fails to enforce focus and trade-offs.
- **EVALUATION CRITERIA**:
  - `Trade-off Enforcement`: 100% (Refuses diffuse targeting).
  - `Beachhead Focus`: Selects high-leverage initial niche.

### Scenario 2: Largest Segment is Not the Best Segment
- **INPUT**:
  Intelligence: *"Segment A (Students & Hobbyists) represents 75% of total market volume (TAM: 5M users, $10/yr willingness-to-pay, high churn). Segment B (B2B Boutique Accounting Firms) represents 5% of volume (TAM: 25k firms, $1,200/yr willingness-to-pay, low churn, urgent tax compliance pain). Which should we target?"*
- **EXPECTED STRATEGIST BEHAVIOR**:
  - Selects **Segment B (Accounting Firms)** despite being 15x smaller in user count.
  - Justifies choice via unit economics and problem severity: Segment B offers $30M high-margin ARR potential with lower CAC, urgent compliance pain, and high LTV, whereas Segment A has poor willingness-to-pay, high support costs, and negative unit margins.
  - Frames Segment A as an explicit non-pursuit for the primary campaign.
- **FAILURE CONDITIONS**:
  - Automatically selects Segment A solely because it has the largest raw user count.
  - Fails to compare LTV, churn, and willingness-to-pay economics.
- **EVALUATION CRITERIA**:
  - `Economic Rigor`: Prioritizes customer value and margin over vanity volume.
  - `Targeting Quality`: Selects high-pain, high-value beachhead.

### Scenario 6: High-Revenue Strategy with Accidental Negative Unit Economics
- **INPUT**:
  Growth proposal: *"We can generate $100,000/month in revenue by running paid TikTok ads selling our $20 physical fitness tracker with free worldwide shipping. (Unit Cost: $12, Shipping: $7, Paid CAC: $15)."*
- **EXPECTED STRATEGIST BEHAVIOR**:
  - Performs instant unit economic audit:
    $$\text{Revenue per unit: } \$20 \quad - \quad \text{COGS: } \$12 \quad - \quad \text{Shipping: } \$7 \quad - \quad \text{CAC: } \$15 \quad = \quad -\$14 \text{ Net Loss per unit}$$
  - Diagnoses **Accidental Bad Economics**: Scaling this campaign generates $100k gross revenue while incinerating $70k in net cash losses with no repeat purchase mechanism.
  - Flatly rejects scaling the campaign; re-engineers the offer (e.g. bundling, minimum order threshold, price increase to $49) before spending ad budget.
- **FAILURE CONDITIONS**:
  - Celebrates the $100,000 top-line revenue projection and approves the campaign.
  - Fails to audit contribution margin and CAC payback.
- **EVALUATION CRITERIA**:
  - `Unit Economics Awareness`: Prioritizes contribution margin over vanity revenue.
  - `Offer Restructuring`: Re-engineers pricing and packaging to ensure profitability.

### Scenario 19: Experiment Portfolio Testing Too Many Unrelated Variables

- **FAILURE CONDITIONS**:
  - Approves the 5-variable simultaneous test.
  - Fails to enforce scientific variable isolation in experiment design.
- **EVALUATION CRITERIA**:
  - `Scientific Experiment Design`: Enforces variable isolation.
  - `Learning Extraction Viability`: Ensures results yield actionable institutional memory.

### Scenario 25: Same Strategy Economics Incorrectly Applied Across SaaS, Affiliate, and E-Commerce
- **INPUT**:
  Executive brief: *"We operate three business units: 1. A B2B SaaS tool, 2. A DTC Skincare E-commerce store, 3. An Affiliate Tech Review blog. Strategy proposal: Apply the exact same 30-day free trial funnel and $100 CAC target across all three businesses."*
